Summary of findings

  1. ClinVar lists 80 CYP2C19 variants, and none is labelled pathogenic: its broken alleles are filed as "drug response", and two are common (*2 at 16.8%, *17 at 20.0%). Sec 1
  2. I calibrated CADD into evidence bands with labelled variants pooled from DPYD, then tested the bands on CYP2C19's own known no-function alleles. Fig 1
  3. The bands put *2 and *4 in the "strong, benign" band, so they can't be trusted for this gene, and no uncertain variant was reclassified (33 → 33). Fig 1
  4. A published Alzheimer's risk score works best in Europeans (AUC 0.672) and is at chance in the African-ancestry group (0.493) in the course's simulated data; without its two APOE SNPs every group sits near 0.58. Fig 2
  5. A Neo4j knowledge graph of my genes ranks PECAM1, APOC1, ITGAX, HLA-DRB1 and APOE on top, and finds real drugs on APOB and APLNR and a route from HLA-DRB1 to Parkinson's. Fig 3

Can variant-effect scores classify CYP2C19's uncertain variants?

Method

I fetched ClinVar labels with CADD, REVEL, AlphaMissense and gnomAD frequencies, calibrated CADD against known labels, and tried to reclassify the variants of uncertain significance (VUS).

Does an Alzheimer's risk score work equally well across ancestries?

Method

I applied a published 22-SNP Alzheimer's score (PGS000334), which includes APOE, to 2,336 real genomes from five ancestry groups, then measured how well it separates cases from controls in each group.

Reference: AUC 0.5 is chance. The pooled cutoff flags 10% of everyone combined. Cross-validated AUC: age alone 0.562, age plus score 0.618.

Interpretation: the score works best in the group it was built in. Without APOE every group sits near 0.58, so the drop in the African-ancestry group comes from how the course data simulated APOE's effect, not from APOE biology. One cutoff for everyone would flag 28% of one group and 2% of another.

Once my Alzheimer's evidence is connected in one graph, which genes stand out, and what does the graph add?

Method

I loaded my 15 Alzheimer's genes, with their Modules 2–4 evidence, into a Neo4j database. Around them I added Reactome pathways, drugs, related diseases and HPO symptoms from the Open Targets Platform, and 200 patients from the Module 6 cohort. I queried the graph in Cypher and ranked the genes by fusing PageRank with my own scores.
